**Date:** September 202
**Prepared By:** Abdullahi Abdi Mohamed

# 1. Introduction

The ERA Integrated Project Management System (ERA-IPMS) will affect several individuals and groups within Emergency Response Aid (ERA).

Stakeholders may use the system directly, provide information to be recorded, manage organisational activities, use reports, or benefit from improved information management.

This stakeholder analysis identifies the key stakeholders involved in the development and future use of ERA-IPMS and describes their expected roles and information needs.

# 2. Purpose

The purpose of this stakeholder analysis is to:

1. Identify individuals and groups connected to ERA-IPMS.
2. Understand how each stakeholder may interact with the system.
3. Identify the information and functionality required by different users.
4. Support the design of user roles and access permissions.
5. Ensure that the system reflects ERA's actual operational needs.

# 3. Key Stakeholders

The initial stakeholders identified for ERA-IPMS are:

1. ERA Management.
2. System Owner and Developer.
3. Programme and Project Coordinators.
4. Field Staff.
5. Community Volunteers.
6. Centre Watchmen.
7. Finance Personnel.
8. Monitoring and Evaluation Personnel.
9. Beneficiaries and Persons with Disabilities.
10. Vulnerable Community Members.
11. Partner Organisations and Service Providers.

# 5. Stakeholder Needs

## 5.1 ERA Management

ERA Management requires access to high-level information for decision-making.

Management should be able to view:

* Total beneficiaries.
* Home visits.
* Referrals.
* Follow-up status.
* Poultry information.
* Egg production.
* Farm activities and harvests.
* Organisational and project expenses.
* Staff and volunteer numbers.
* Upcoming and pending activities.
* Reports.

Management requires summary information rather than responsibility for entering all operational records.

## 5.2 System Owner and Developer

The system owner and developer requires the ability to:

* Design and develop the system.
* Configure system settings.
* Test functionality.
* Manage technical issues.
* Maintain and improve the system.
* Manage authorised administrative access where appropriate.
* Protect system source code and technical assets.

The ownership and administration of the production system will be governed by future organisational and technical arrangements.

## 5.3 Programme and Project Coordinators

Programme and Project Coordinators require the ability to:

* Manage projects.
* Plan activities.
* Record activities.
* Monitor activity progress.
* Review beneficiary services.
* Monitor referrals and follow-ups.
* Assign responsibilities.
* Generate project-related reports.

## 5.4 Field Staff

Field Staff may require access to:

* Beneficiary registration.
* Disability assessments.
* Home visit records.
* Referrals.
* Follow-up records.
* Assigned activities.

Field Staff should only access information relevant to their authorised responsibilities.

## 5.5 Community Volunteers

Community Volunteers support ERA's activities at the community level.

Their responsibilities may include:

* Conducting or supporting home visits.
* Following up on referrals.
* Supporting community activities.
* Providing relevant field information.

Volunteer access should be limited according to assigned responsibilities and data protection requirements.

## 5.6 Centre Watchmen

Centre Watchmen currently support small farm activities.

They may need to record or report:

* Planting activities.
* Watering.
* Weeding.
* Harvesting.
* Other assigned farm activities.

Their system access should be limited to farm-related activities assigned to them.

## 5.7 Finance Personnel

Finance Personnel require access to:

* Organisational expenses.
* Project expenses.
* Poultry expenses.
* Farm expenses.
* Financial records.
* Relevant financial reports.

Financial access should be restricted to authorised users.

## 5.8 Monitoring and Evaluation Personnel

Monitoring and Evaluation Personnel require information that supports:

* Activity monitoring.
* Beneficiary monitoring.
* Referral monitoring.
* Poultry monitoring.
* Farm monitoring.
* Indicator tracking.
* Report preparation.

They may require access to dashboards, summaries, and authorised operational records.

## 5.9 Beneficiaries and Persons with Disabilities

Beneficiaries and persons with disabilities are important stakeholders because the system will manage information related to services provided to them.

The initial version of ERA-IPMS will not provide direct system access to beneficiaries.

Their information must be managed responsibly and protected from unauthorised access.

## 5.10 Vulnerable Community Members

Vulnerable community members may participate in or benefit from relevant ERA activities.

The system may contain information relating to services and activities involving vulnerable persons.

The initial version will not provide direct user access to these individuals.

## 5.11 Partner Organisations and Service Providers

Partner organisations and service providers may support ERA through:

* Referral services.
* Project partnerships.
* Technical support.
* Programme collaboration.

The initial version of ERA-IPMS will not provide direct access to external partners.

Future versions may consider secure partner access or data-sharing features where appropriate.

# 8. Stakeholder Expectations

The main expectations of stakeholders include:

* A system that is easy to use.
* Clear and organised information.
* Faster access to records.
* Improved monitoring.
* Better reporting.
* Appropriate protection of beneficiary and organisational information.
* User access based on responsibilities.
* A system that can grow with ERA.

# 9. Stakeholder Risks and Considerations

The following factors should be considered during development:

1. Users may have different levels of digital literacy.
2. Training may be required before deployment.
3. Internet availability may affect access to a web-based system.
4. Beneficiary information requires appropriate confidentiality and access controls.
5. System features should be introduced gradually to avoid unnecessary complexity.
6. Stakeholders should validate major workflows before final implementation.
7. User feedback should be collected during testing.

# 10. Conclusion

The stakeholder analysis identifies the key individuals and groups connected to ERA-IPMS.

Different stakeholders have different responsibilities, information needs, and levels of system access. These findings will guide the next stage of development: the definition of detailed user roles, permissions, and access control requirements.

ERA Management and authorised system administrators will require broader access to management information, while field staff, volunteers, finance personnel, and farm personnel will require access based on their assigned responsibilities.

The stakeholder analysis will therefore serve as an input to the User Roles and Permissions document and the Software Requirements Specification.
