**Project:** ERA Integrated Project Management System (ERA-IPMS)
**Document Type:** Stakeholder Analysis
**Version:** 1.1
**Date:** September 2026
**Prepared By:** Abdullahi Abdi Mohamed

# 1. Introduction

The ERA Integrated Project Management System (ERA-IPMS) will affect several individuals and groups connected to Emergency Response Aid (ERA).

Stakeholders may use the system directly, provide information to be recorded, manage organisational activities, perform operational responsibilities, use reports, receive services, or benefit from improved information management.

This stakeholder analysis identifies the key stakeholders involved in the development and future use of ERA-IPMS and describes their interests, responsibilities, information needs, influence, and expected interaction with the system.

The document is aligned with the current ERA-IPMS Concept Note, Needs Assessment, and README baseline.

# 2. Purpose

The purpose of this stakeholder analysis is to:

1. Identify individuals and groups connected to ERA-IPMS.
2. Understand how each stakeholder may interact with the system.
3. Identify the information and functionality relevant to different stakeholders.
4. Support the design of user titles, responsibilities, permissions, and access controls.
5. Identify stakeholders who should participate in requirements validation and testing.
6. Ensure that the system reflects ERA's actual operational needs.
7. Establish a basis for stakeholder communication during development and deployment.

# 3. Stakeholder Categories

ERA-IPMS stakeholders can be grouped into the following broad categories:

### 3.1 Internal Management Stakeholders

* ERA Management.
* Director.
* Programme Coordinator.
* Finance personnel.
* System Administrator.

### 3.2 Operational Stakeholders

* Members.
* Field personnel.
* Community volunteers.
* Centre watchmen and farm personnel.
* Personnel assigned to poultry responsibilities.
* Personnel assigned to monitoring and evaluation responsibilities.

### 3.3 Service and Community Stakeholders

* Beneficiaries.
* Persons with disabilities.
* Vulnerable community members.
* Partner organisations.
* Referral service providers.

### 3.4 Technical Stakeholders

* System Owner and Developer.
* Future technical support personnel.

Stakeholder categories describe organisational involvement and responsibilities. They do not automatically represent system titles.

# 4. Approved System Title Model

ERA-IPMS has five initial system titles:

1. **Admin**
2. **Director**
3. **Programme Coordinator**
4. **Finance**
5. **Member**

The stakeholder analysis must not treat every operational function as a separate system title.

For example:

* Field work may be an assigned responsibility of a Member.
* Community volunteer work may be an assigned responsibility of a Member.
* Poultry work may be an assigned responsibility of a Member.
* Farm work may be an assigned responsibility of a Member.
* M&E-related responsibilities may be assigned according to organisational requirements and permissions.

A user's title, permissions, and responsibilities are separate concepts.

# 7. Stakeholder Needs

## 7.1 ERA Management

ERA Management requires high-level information to support organisational decision-making and oversight.

Management information may include:

* Total beneficiaries.
* Disability service information.
* Home visits.
* Referrals.
* Follow-up status.
* Project information.
* Activity progress.
* Poultry information.
* Egg production.
* Farm activities and harvests.
* Organisational and project expenses.
* Staff and volunteer information.
* Upcoming activities.
* Pending activities.
* Reports.

Management generally requires summary and oversight information rather than responsibility for entering all operational records.

# 7.2 System Owner and Developer

The System Owner and Developer requires the ability to:

* Design and develop the system.
* Maintain the application.
* Configure authorised technical settings.
* Test system functionality.
* Investigate technical issues.
* Maintain source code and technical assets.
* Perform authorised system administration.
* Support security and maintenance activities.

Technical access must be governed separately from programme decision-making authority.

Technical authority does not automatically provide programme approval authority.

# 7.3 Admin

The Admin is responsible for system administration.

The Admin may be responsible for:

* User administration.
* Creating and managing system titles.
* Assigning permissions.
* Managing user access.
* Maintaining system-level configuration where authorised.
* Managing selected project administration functions.
* Supporting system accountability.
* Reviewing audit information where authorised.

The Admin title does not automatically provide unrestricted access to all programme information. Permissions and record-level access remain applicable.

# 7.4 Director

The Director provides organisational and programme oversight.

Responsibilities may include:

* Reviewing programme performance.
* Reviewing projects and activities.
* Providing programme oversight.
* Reviewing monitoring and evaluation information.
* Supporting continuation or modification of projects.
* Reviewing management reports.
* Supporting organisational decision-making.

The Director generally reviews and oversees information rather than directly entering all operational records.

# 7.5 Programme Coordinator

The Programme Coordinator is responsible for programme and project coordination.

Responsibilities may include:

* Coordinating projects.
* Planning activities.
* Monitoring implementation.
* Assigning responsibilities.
* Reviewing beneficiary services.
* Reviewing home visits.
* Monitoring referrals and follow-ups.
* Coordinating operational activities.
* Monitoring poultry and farm activities where relevant.
* Preparing or reviewing project reports.

The Programme Coordinator's detailed permissions will be defined in the User Roles and Permissions and Software Requirements Specification documents.

# 7.6 Finance

The Finance title is responsible for authorised financial information.

Responsibilities may include:

* Recording organisational expenses.
* Recording project expenses.
* Recording poultry expenses.
* Recording farm expenses.
* Recording authorised sales or income.
* Reviewing financial records.
* Preparing authorised financial reports.

Financial information must be restricted to authorised users.

# 7.7 Members

Member is the general operational title for users who perform assigned organisational or programme responsibilities.

Members may be assigned responsibilities such as:

* Disability services.
* Beneficiary registration.
* Disability assessment.
* Home visits.
* Referral creation and submission.
* Referral follow-up.
* Community activities.
* Project activities.
* Poultry operations.
* Farm operations.
* Other validated organisational responsibilities.

A Member's access is determined by assigned permissions and responsibilities.

Members should generally have access to their own authorised operational records and relevant records they personally registered, assessed, or visited, subject to the final access-control rules.

Members may create and submit referrals where authorised. Approval must be performed by an authorised person where approval is required.

# 7.8 Field and Community Personnel

Field and community personnel support direct service delivery.

Their responsibilities may include:

* Registering beneficiaries.
* Conducting assessments.
* Conducting home visits.
* Creating referrals.
* Conducting referral follow-ups.
* Recording service outcomes.
* Supporting community activities.
* Providing relevant field information.

These functions may be performed by Members with the appropriate responsibilities and permissions.

Access should be limited to information relevant to the user's authorised responsibilities.

# 7.9 Farm Personnel and Centre Watchmen

Farm personnel currently support small farm operations.

Their responsibilities may include:

* Planting.
* Watering.
* Weeding.
* Harvesting.
* Recording farm activities.
* Recording harvest information.
* Reporting farm activities.
* Supporting the transfer of farm produce to poultry operations.

Where these personnel use ERA-IPMS, their access should be limited to authorised farm-related functions.

# 7.10 Poultry Personnel

Poultry personnel may be assigned responsibility for operational poultry activities.

Responsibilities may include:

* Recording poultry stock.
* Recording poultry purchases.
* Recording egg production.
* Recording feed usage.
* Recording purchased feed.
* Recording feed received from farm produce.
* Recording poultry illnesses.
* Recording poultry deaths.
* Recording poultry sales.
* Recording other authorised poultry activities.

Poultry responsibilities may be assigned to Members rather than requiring a separate system title.

# 7.11 Monitoring and Evaluation Stakeholders

Monitoring and evaluation is an organisational responsibility rather than one of the five initial system titles.

M&E responsibilities may include:

* Monitoring programme activities.
* Monitoring beneficiary services.
* Reviewing referrals and follow-ups.
* Monitoring project activities.
* Reviewing poultry and farm information.
* Tracking indicators.
* Reviewing programme performance.
* Preparing monitoring information and reports.

The exact allocation of M&E permissions will be determined during the Software Requirements Specification and User Roles and Permissions stages.

The Director has programme and M&E oversight responsibilities in the current project baseline.

# 7.12 Beneficiaries and Persons with Disabilities

Beneficiaries and persons with disabilities are important stakeholders because ERA-IPMS will manage information related to services provided to them.

They may:

* Provide information during registration or assessment.
* Receive disability-related services.
* Receive referrals.
* Receive follow-up services.
* Participate in relevant activities.

The initial version of ERA-IPMS will not provide direct system access to beneficiaries.

Their information must be protected from unauthorised access.

# 7.13 Vulnerable Community Members

Vulnerable community members may participate in or benefit from ERA programmes and activities.

The system may contain information relating to services or activities involving vulnerable persons.

The initial version will not provide direct user access to these individuals.

# 7.14 Partner Organisations and Service Providers

Partner organisations and service providers may support ERA through:

* Referral services.
* Project partnerships.
* Technical support.
* Programme collaboration.
* Service delivery.

The initial version of ERA-IPMS will not provide direct external-partner access.

Future secure partner access or data-sharing capabilities may be considered only after validated requirements and appropriate data-protection arrangements are established.

# 10. Stakeholder Expectations

The main stakeholder expectations include:

* A system that is practical and easy to use.
* Clear and organised information.
* Faster access to authorised records.
* Improved monitoring.
* Better referral and follow-up tracking.
* Improved project and activity visibility.
* Better poultry and farm monitoring.
* Improved financial information management.
* Better reporting.
* Appropriate protection of beneficiary and organisational information.
* Access based on responsibilities and permissions.
* A system that can grow with validated organisational needs.

# 11. Stakeholder Risks and Considerations

The following factors should be considered during development:

1. Users may have different levels of digital literacy.
2. Appropriate training may be required before deployment.
3. Internet availability may affect access to a web-based system.
4. Beneficiary information requires appropriate confidentiality and access controls.
5. Users should only receive permissions necessary for their responsibilities.
6. Record-level access may be required for sensitive operational information.
7. System features should be introduced gradually to avoid unnecessary complexity.
8. Stakeholders should validate major workflows before final implementation.
9. User feedback should be collected during testing.
10. Stakeholder responsibilities may change over time and should not require unnecessary creation of new system titles.
11. Future titles may be created by an authorised Admin where organisational requirements justify them.
12. Programme decision-making authority should remain distinct from technical administration.
13. External stakeholders should not receive system access without explicit requirements, authorisation, and appropriate data-protection controls.
14. The system should avoid collecting information that is not required for a validated organisational purpose.

# 14. Conclusion

The stakeholder analysis identifies the individuals and groups connected to ERA-IPMS and establishes their expected interests, responsibilities, information needs, influence, and participation in the project.

The analysis confirms that ERA-IPMS must distinguish between organisational stakeholders, system titles, permissions, and operational responsibilities.

The five initial system titles are Admin, Director, Programme Coordinator, Finance, and Member. Operational responsibilities such as field work, community support, poultry, farm operations, and monitoring and evaluation should be assigned according to validated organisational needs rather than automatically creating separate system titles.

Beneficiaries, persons with disabilities, vulnerable community members, partner organisations, and service providers are important stakeholders but will not receive direct system access in the initial version.

The stakeholder analysis will serve as an input to the Software Requirements Specification and User Roles and Permissions documents and will support structured stakeholder validation throughout development.
